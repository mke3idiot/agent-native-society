# Agent Native Society — business plan v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Status:** strategic hypothesis, not a forecast or investment solicitation  
**Primary outcome:** earned authorship and durable public recognition for the author.  
**Operating thesis:** turn a public body of thought into evidence through real, bounded implementation work; turn repeated evidence into reusable Agent Ready infrastructure.

## 1. Executive summary

Agent Native Society is a public research-to-implementation venture: it defines a future in which AI agents become accountable service users; develops AI Optimization as the migration method; and makes Agent Ready a practical capability of services. It does not compete to own MCP, A2A, UCP, or an agent runtime.

The near-term business is not “build the society.” It is to help a small number of organizations answer a pressing question: **what must change for an authorized AI agent to safely use this service, and how do we prove it?** The first paid/funded work should be a tightly bounded assessment and pilot. Repeated patterns become an open reference architecture and, only when justified, productized policy/evidence tooling.

## 2. The author-brand thesis

### Objective

Be known as the person who made a coherent, protocol-independent case for the transition from human-only services to accountable agent-mediated services—and who demonstrated it with honest, technically credible artifacts.

### What creates credible name recognition

| Asset | Public proof | Distribution channel | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding corpus | manifesto, prior art, architecture, version history | GitHub, technical writing, talks | rhetoric without evidence |
| Reference implementation | executable demo, tests, threat model, design decisions | GitHub, demos, peer review | toy demo mistaken for production |
| Field learning | anonymized case-study patterns, what failed, migration playbook | workshops, essays, conference proposals | confidentiality prevents reusable learning |
| Repeatable method | Agent Ready assessment and maturity profile | paid pilots, partner referrals | empty badge/certification theater |
| Institutional contribution | open profiles, working groups, public critique | standards/community participation | captured by a protocol/vendor |

### Non-negotiable brand rule

Never state or imply that the author invented the category, owns a standard, has customers, or has achieved readiness/compliance without evidence. The public advantage is intellectual honesty plus execution traceability.

## 3. Problem and initial customer

### Problem hypothesis

Organizations can expose tools to agents but still lack a credible answer to: who authorized the action, what did the agent commit to, what policy stopped an unsafe action, what evidence remains, and what happens when it fails. Generic protocol integration does not answer these operational questions.

### Beachhead customer profile

Start with a service operator that has all of the following:

- a repeated, digitally mediated workflow with a clear provider and user/principal;
- a meaningful but bounded commitment (quote, booking request, purchase request, change request), not safety-critical physical execution;
- existing API or structured backend capability;
- painful manual coordination or a strong strategic need to support agents;
- someone who owns workflow operations and can sponsor a 4–8 week pilot;
- permission to publish at least anonymized lessons or an open derived artifact.

Candidate domains are B2B procurement requests, booking/change workflows, enterprise service operations, or regulated-adjacent back-office flows. Consumer payments, healthcare decisions, and irreversible physical action are explicitly not beachheads.

## 4. Offer ladder

| Stage | Offer | Buyer outcome | Evidence produced | Commercial hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | public corpus and ARCG sandbox | shared language, technical credibility | open artifacts | free distribution |
| 1 | Agent Ready discovery workshop | map commitment boundaries and migration value | decision brief | paid or strategic partner-sponsored |
| 2 | Agent Ready assessment | capability inventory, maturity view, risk and migration roadmap | assessment report + ADR | fixed-scope fee |
| 3 | bounded pilot | one governed agent-mediated workflow | before/after metrics, threat model, reference pattern | project fee |
| 4 | implementation partnership | adapters, policy/evidence integration, operations handoff | reusable modules where permitted | implementation/support revenue |
| 5 | shared infrastructure | conformance tooling, policy/evidence components, sector profiles | adoption and interoperability evidence | subscription, support, or consortium model |

No stage should be offered before the preceding evidence exists. In particular, do not sell certification or compliance claims in v0.1.

## 5. Product strategy

### Wedge: Agent Ready Capability Gateway

The ARCG reference implementation is the proof object. It must show a capability contract, explicit delegation, deterministic policy decisions, a replaceable protocol adapter, append-only evidence, and a recourse path. Its success criterion is not agent cleverness; it is a comprehensible, governed commitment flow.

### Productization sequence

1. Publish sandbox and threat model.
2. Build an assessment worksheet/maturity profile from the sandbox's concrete boundaries.
3. Run one design-partner assessment; record the learning as an anonymized ADR/case pattern if allowed.
4. Implement one narrowly scoped pilot.
5. Extract only repeated, stable primitives into libraries or a hosted service.

This sequence prevents premature platforming while building public proof at every stage.

## 6. Go-to-market and reputation engine

### Narrative

Do not market “AI agents can do everything.” Market the sharper question: **when an agent acts on behalf of someone, what makes that action legitimate, inspectable, and recoverable?**

### Distribution motions

- Publish a concise Japanese and English thesis derived from the repository, each linked to evidence rather than promotional claims.
- Release ARCG with a walkthrough that demonstrates both allowed and denied actions.
- Interview 15–20 potential design partners; seek disconfirming evidence as actively as demand signals.
- Offer a small number of invitation-only architecture sessions to operators, security leaders, and platform teams.
- Submit talks or essays that contrast agent capability with agent authority, using prior art respectfully.
- Join relevant standards/community discussions as a contributor of concrete profiles, tests, or critiques—not as an owner of the future.

### What not to do

- buy attention with unsupported futurist claims;
- launch a generic “AI agency” or MCP integration shop;
- announce a platform, marketplace, or certification program before field evidence;
- hide prior art or use vague language to manufacture novelty.

## 7. Operating model

Initially operate as a public research and implementation practice. Keep the code and canonical documents in this repository; isolate client-specific material in private repositories or access-controlled systems; publish only with explicit permission. Each engagement must have a problem statement, scope, risk boundary, decision record, success metrics, and reusable-learning decision.

| Role | Initial owner | Trigger to add capacity |
|---|---|---|
| thesis, product direction, public authorship | author | remains author-led |
| research, implementation, documentation | author + Codex | repeated delivery workload or specialist need |
| security/privacy/legal review | qualified external specialist | before any real sensitive, payment, regulated, or high-impact pilot |
| sales/design partners | author | after sandbox demonstrates a credible workflow |

## 8. Milestones and decision gates

### First 30 days: make the thesis executable

- implement ARCG with the acceptance criteria already recorded;
- publish a threat model and a five-minute reproducible demonstration;
- define Agent Ready maturity levels as a hypothesis, not certification;
- publish a one-page design-partner brief.

**Gate:** can an informed third party explain why the policy layer, not the prompt, controls a commitment?

### Days 31–90: discover a buyer and falsify the wedge

- conduct 15–20 structured interviews across at least two candidate verticals;
- document pains, existing alternatives, decision makers, security constraints, and willingness to run a pilot;
- select one design partner only if a workflow, sponsor, data boundary, and public-learning path exist;
- write ADRs for vertical selection and the pilot boundary.

**Gate:** is there a sponsor willing to exchange time, access, or money for a bounded assessment/pilot—not merely praise the idea?

### Months 4–12: earn repeatability

- deliver one assessment and one pilot, with legal/security review as warranted;
- publish an anonymized case pattern or a clearly marked “unable to publish” lesson;
- extract a second adapter or independent provider integration;
- decide whether the repeatable value is methodology, implementation partnership, tooling, or none.

**Gate:** does repeated evidence show a problem larger than one bespoke integration?

## 9. Metrics

### Leading indicators for earned authorship

- externally initiated technical conversations with relevant operators/researchers;
- substantive review, issues, citations, or contributions to public artifacts;
- invitations to explain or critique the framework based on the work itself;
- independent reproduction or adaptation of the reference implementation.

### Validation indicators

- number of interviewees with a concrete workflow and budget/sponsor;
- assessment-to-pilot conversion;
- change in manual steps, turnaround time, exception rate, and explainability for the selected workflow;
- percentage of commitment attempts correctly denied by policy tests;
- reusable primitives extracted across independent engagements.

### Guardrail indicators

- public claims with source links;
- unresolved critical security/recourse gaps;
- proportion of client work that produces reusable learning;
- concentration of dependency on a single vendor, protocol, or client.

Vanity metrics (followers, impressions, generic stars) may be observed but are not success criteria without evidence of relevant engagement.

## 10. Financial model (hypotheses only)

Do not begin with a venture-scale forecast. The first financial goal is to fund credible learning without turning every hour into unreusable custom work.

- **Revenue hypothesis:** fixed-scope workshops/assessments → bounded implementation pilots → implementation/support → reusable tooling or consortium support.
- **Cost hypothesis:** author time is the primary initial cost; later costs are specialist review, security assurance, hosting, and integration maintenance.
- **Unit-economics test:** an engagement is attractive only if it pays for delivery *and* produces reusable evidence, components, or distribution—or intentionally purchases a strategically critical learning.
- **Stop condition:** if three consecutive qualified conversations do not identify a sponsor, workflow, and measurable benefit, do not scale sales activity; revisit the wedge.

## 11. Risks and countermeasures

| Risk | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Large companies copy vocabulary | make public value depend on depth of method, artifacts, and field learning, not terminology |
| Protocol churn | canonical semantics + adapter architecture; explicitly test portability |
| Security incident damages reputation | sandbox first; no real credentials/payments; independent review before sensitive pilots |
| No willingness to pay | interview before productizing; keep offer ladder reversible |
| Consulting trap | require a reuse/public-learning decision at engagement start |
| Public thesis becomes stale | versioned amendments and published disconfirmation |
| Legal/regulatory exposure | work within low-risk sandbox boundaries and use qualified counsel before regulated deployment |

## 12. Decisions needed from the author

No decision is required to begin the sandbox, research, and public writing. Before external branding or commercial outreach, the author must confirm:

1. exact Japanese and Romanized public name;
2. whether public outreach is under the individual name, a company name, or both;
3. acceptable visibility and time commitment for interviews, talks, and partner work.
