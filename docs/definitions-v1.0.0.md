# Definitions v1.0.0

**Agent Native Society** — The desired societal condition in which AI agents can discover, understand, request, use, and be held accountable for use of digital and real-world services under legitimate delegated authority. It includes institutions, incentives, law, interface design, identity, and recourse—not only software.

**AI Optimization (AI最適化)** — A migration methodology for transforming existing social systems and services so that agent-mediated use becomes safer, more understandable, more auditable, and more beneficial. It is not merely model optimization or workflow automation.

**Agent Ready** — A property of a service or system that is safely usable by a suitably authorized AI agent. It implies machine-legible capabilities and constraints, scoped authority, explicit consent, predictable execution, observability, reversibility or redress where appropriate, and abuse resistance.

**Agent** — A software-based actor that can pursue a delegated objective by reasoning, invoking capabilities, and producing or requesting actions. An agent need not be fully autonomous; human approval may be required at any boundary.

**Principal** — The person, organization, or other recognized entity on whose behalf an agent acts.

**Delegation** — A bounded grant of authority from a principal to an agent, including scope, purpose, time, spend/risk limits, approval conditions, and revocation path.

**Service provider** — A party exposing a capability, whether a digital API, physical operation, marketplace, public service, or another agent.

**Protocol** — A replaceable technical mechanism for discovery, communication, context exchange, transactions, or coordination. A protocol does not itself establish legitimacy or accountability.
