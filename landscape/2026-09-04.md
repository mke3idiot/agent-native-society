# Market landscape — 2026-09-04

This landscape is a design map, not investment advice or endorsement. Markets and standards change quickly; sources and claims should be revisited before making commercial decisions.

| Segment | What is emerging | Strategic question for Agent Native Society |
|---|---|---|
| Agent runtimes & builders | agents that plan, browse, call tools, and orchestrate workflows | How is authority bounded separately from model capability? |
| Enterprise systems | agent access to CRM, ERP, support, analytics, and operations | Can legacy permissions, audit, and exception handling survive agent-mediated access? |
| Developer interoperability | MCP, APIs, tool schemas, gateways | Can capabilities be portable and meaningful beyond a single vendor's runtime? |
| Agent interoperability | A2A and analogous messaging/discovery approaches | How do agents negotiate responsibilities, failure, and escalation across organizations? |
| Commerce | UCP and other agentic shopping/payment stacks | How are offers, intent, authorization, consumer protection, and post-purchase support represented? |
| Identity, authorization & payments | OAuth-derived delegation, verifiable credentials, payment authorization | How do we avoid credential sharing while making delegation inspectable and revocable? |
| Security & observability | tool security, prompt-injection defenses, policy engines, audit systems | What evidence proves an action was authorized and safely performed? |
| Public and physical services | booking, logistics, healthcare-adjacent, civic and operational systems | Which actions require human oversight, due process, or special safety controls? |

## Opportunity thesis

The opportunity is broader than building another agent or protocol. It is to help services become Agent Ready: clarify capability semantics; separate inquiry from commitment; issue bounded authorization; implement policy and evidence; and provide recourse. This creates value for service providers, principals, and intermediaries only if it reduces real coordination cost without externalizing fraud, privacy, or safety risk.

## Adoption sequence

Near-term adoption is likely to begin with bounded, reversible, high-volume tasks: retrieval, comparison, drafting, support triage, and workflow preparation. Higher-impact transactions require stronger delegation, commercial and legal arrangements, security evaluation, and dispute operations. Physical-world execution is a separate safety frontier.

## Risks to avoid

- treating a protocol integration as proof of safety or accountability;
- optimizing conversion while obscuring user choice and financial commitment;
- creating irreversible vendor gatekeepers around identity or discovery;
- exposing privileged tools to untrusted content or prompt injection;
- presenting autonomous behavior as a substitute for clear responsibility.
